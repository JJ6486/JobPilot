# What to return
1. `jd`: parse the JD.
   - role_family: "software_ict" for software / IT / ICT engineering or technical roles; "teaching" for teaching or training roles (including ICT, computer science and maths teachers); "other" otherwise.
   - must_have_skills / nice_to_have_skills: short names of skills, tools, qualifications or experience types. Items under "required", "must", "qualifications" are must-haves; "plus", "preferred", "nice to have", "extra points" are nice-to-haves.
   - hard_conditions: only conditions the JD EXPLICITLY states, quoted as closely as possible in the JD's own words: nationality ("Saudi nationals only"), visa/iqama transfer, gender, a required location with no remote option, a licence or specific qualification the JD says is required ("must hold a valid teaching licence", "proven teaching qualifications"), driving licence. NEVER include years of experience (scored separately), degrees (education_requirements), skills, or anything the JD doesn't say. If none, return an empty list.
   - remote_allowed: true only if the JD says remote or hybrid-from-anywhere is possible.
   - Leave unknown fields empty. Copy contact details exactly as written.
2. `requirements`: one judgement per must-have and nice-to-have item. Name each `requirement` with a SHORT canonical skill or qualification name (1-4 words, e.g. "Docker", "Microservices", "Saudi consulting experience", "Teaching qualification", "Child safeguarding") so the same skill gets the same name across different jobs; put the JD's own wording in `note`.
   - "matched": the truth bank has this skill at level core or project (by name or alias), or a bullet/project clearly proves it. `evidence_ids` MUST list real ids from the truth bank (bullet ids like exp_1_b2, project ids, workshop ids, education ids, certification ids). For language, visa, nationality or location requirements, cite the constraint field instead: "constraints.languages", "constraints.visa_status", "constraints.nationality", "constraints.accepted_cities". Set matched_skill to the truth-bank skill name if one applies.
   - "adjacent": the candidate lacks it but has a closely related skill (it appears in that skill's adjacent_to, or is an obvious sibling, e.g. Vue vs React). Put the related skill in matched_skill and its evidence in evidence_ids.
   - "missing": anything else, including skills that are only at level "learning" and anything listed in never_claim.
   - A requirement that bundles several items ("CI/CD and containerization", "React, Angular or Vue" is NOT bundled because it's a choice): "matched" only if the candidate has EVERY item; if some are missing, use "adjacent" and say which part is missing in the note.
   - Degree requirements: matched only if the truth-bank education satisfies it. Teaching qualifications (B.Ed, PGCE, teaching licence, TESOL/CELTA as a teaching credential) are missing unless the truth bank lists one.
3. `relevant_years`: years of professional experience relevant to THIS role, computed from experience start/end dates (end "Present" = today). For teaching roles, count actual teaching employment in full, and count teaching-related experience as PARTIAL teaching experience at about half its duration: work roles with training duties (e.g. a "Trainer" title or bullets about training students) and "activity" entries (workshops, mentoring, student programmes). Count overlapping periods only once. Say in experience_note how much is formal teaching vs partial.
   `experience_fit`: strong / partial / weak versus the JD's level and years. Explain in experience_note.
   For teaching roles without any formal classroom teaching employment, experience_fit is at most "partial" (and "partial" when the partial teaching experience is substantial).
4. `role_alignment`: strong / partial / weak: how close the candidate's actual roles and titles are to this job. For teaching roles, a candidate whose roles include training, mentoring or workshop facilitation in the subject is "partial" (not "weak"). Explain in role_alignment_note.
5. `hard_conditions`: one judgement per item in jd.hard_conditions (copy the condition text exactly). Set `category`: "nationality", "visa" (iqama/transfer), "gender", "location" (must be on-site somewhere specific), "licence", "qualification" (degree, teaching qualification, certification), or "other". "not_met" when the candidate clearly does not satisfy it; "unclear" when the truth bank can't tell or the JD wording is ambiguous (e.g. "teaching qualifications (Bachelor/Masters)" could mean a subject degree). Give a one-sentence reason.
6. `logistics`: location (accepted_cities, open_to_relocation, open_to_remote vs the job's city and remote_allowed), visa/iqama, licence, language, schedule. Only judge factors the JD actually mentions or implies (always judge location). Status ok / concern / blocker; use "blocker" only for something the JD explicitly requires.
7. `red_flags`: short phrases for anything worrying (e.g. "requires 5+ years, candidate has ~3", "Saudi nationals only").
8. `summary`: exactly three short lines (max ~20 words each): overall fit, biggest strength, biggest gap or risk.
